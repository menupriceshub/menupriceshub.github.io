Overview

Commander's Palace is a Creole restaurant in the Garden District of New Orleans, and anyone looking up Commander's Palace menu prices usually wants to know what to order first. The short answer is turtle soup, Creole-style seafood, and a famous bread pudding soufflé.

Turtle soup is the dish many first-time guests try. It is rich, savory, and finished with a splash of sherry, which gives it a deep, slightly tangy taste. Gulf seafood, such as shrimp and fish, often shows up with buttery sauces and Creole spices. Pecan-crusted fish and shrimp dishes are also common picks.

The Commander's Palace menu changes with the seasons, so not every dish is always available. Soups, appetizers, and entrees follow a Southern and Creole style, with plenty of sauce, spice, and comfort. For dessert, the bread pudding soufflé is the signature: warm, soft, and sweet, served with a whiskey sauce.

The restaurant has been part of New Orleans dining since 1893. It is easy to spot by its turquoise-and-white building with a striped awning. Inside, the mood is polished and festive, with attentive service. It suits birthdays, family celebrations, and special dinners. Weekend jazz brunch is also a popular tradition here.

Plan ahead and book a table, and check the dress code before you go. Order a soup to start, share an appetizer, and save room for the soufflé.
