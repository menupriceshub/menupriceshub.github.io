Overview

Pat's King of Steaks is a South Philadelphia icon, and anyone checking Pat's King of Steaks menu prices is usually planning a proper Philly cheesesteak. It stands at the corner of 9th Street and Passyunk Avenue and is known as the place that put the cheesesteak on the map.

The story goes back to 1930, when Pat Olivieri and his brother Harry ran a hot dog stand and, as the legend goes, tried chopped steak on a roll. That experiment turned into the sandwich Philadelphia is now famous for. What sets Pat's apart is its open-air, walk-up setup, where you order at the window and eat standing or at the outdoor tables.

The Pat's King of Steaks menu is built around the cheesesteak: thinly sliced ribeye piled onto a soft, chewy Italian roll, juicy and savory with griddled edges. Cheese choices usually include Cheez Whiz, provolone, or American, and you can add fried onions. Cheese fries and a cold drink are the classic sides.

The vibe is loud, fast, and street-corner casual, so it suits quick bites, late-night cravings, and first-time visitors exploring Philadelphia. It is a walk-up spot with no drive-thru and no formal dining room, and it is well known as a late-night stop.

Know your order before you reach the window, since the line moves quickly. Locals say "wit" for with onions and "witout" for without, followed by your cheese.
