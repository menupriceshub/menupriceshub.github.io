Overview

Duarte's Tavern menu prices are part of a Pescadero tradition that began in 1894, when Frank Duarte established the business as a saloon and barbershop. Food was soon added for local residents and visitors, and the Duarte family has continued the restaurant through four generations. Today, the tavern still reflects the family's Portuguese roots and its long connection to the coastal farming community.

The Duarte's Tavern menu brings together American country cooking with seafood and Portuguese influences. Local fish changes with the weekly catch, while favorites such as cream of artichoke soup, crab cioppino, crab sandwiches, and fresh pies have become closely associated with the restaurant. Produce is also grown in the restaurant's own garden or sourced from nearby growers, keeping the food closely tied to Pescadero.

Set along Stage Road, Duarte's has the comfortable character of an old country tavern rather than a polished modern restaurant. The setting works well for a relaxed meal with family or friends, especially after exploring the nearby coastal farming area. The restaurant's original tavern bar, which survived the fire that destroyed much of Pescadero in the late 1920s, remains part of the historic interior.

One especially memorable piece of the story is the cream of artichoke soup. Frank Duarte's grandson Ron Duarte and his wife Lynn first experimented with the recipe in the early 1960s, and Ron's handwritten recipe book is still kept in the kitchen. If you are visiting for the first time, the artichoke soup alongside a seafood specialty or a slice of the tavern's traditional pie offers a good introduction to its cooking.
