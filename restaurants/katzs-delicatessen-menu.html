Overview

Katz's Delicatessen menu prices interest many visitors to New York's Lower East Side, where this classic Jewish deli has stood at 205 E Houston St for well over a century. It is best known for hand-carved pastrami on rye and is often counted among the city's true food landmarks.

The deli began in 1888 as Iceland Brothers on Ludlow Street, and became Katz's Delicatessen after Willy Katz and his cousin Benny took over. It later moved across the street to its present location during subway construction. The business was family run for generations, and its wartime slogan, "Send a Salami to Your Boy in the Army," is still part of its story.

The pastrami sandwich is the star, with thick, peppery slices piled between rye bread and a swipe of mustard. The deli says its corned beef and pastrami are cured slowly, for up to 30 days, without pumping or additives. Brisket, hot dogs, matzo ball soup, knishes and classic deli sides are also popular, and the sandwiches are large enough that many groups share one.

The location suits a food stop while exploring the Lower East Side. It tends to get busy at lunchtime and on weekends, so off-peak visits usually mean shorter lines. You receive a paper ticket at the door, order at the counter, and pay on the way out, so keep the ticket safe.
