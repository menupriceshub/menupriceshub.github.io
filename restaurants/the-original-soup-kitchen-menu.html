Overview

The Original Soup Kitchen has a New York story that goes back to 1984, when Al Yeganeh opened Soup Kitchen International at 55th Street and Eighth Avenue in Midtown Manhattan. The small storefront became known for its intensely focused approach to soup, with Yeganeh developing a rotating selection of recipes made with fresh ingredients.

Rather than offering a broad restaurant menu, the kitchen built its identity around soup. Past selections have included Lobster Bisque, Jambalaya, Crab Bisque, Mulligatawny, Chicken Vegetable, and other changing creations. The variety could move from familiar comfort soups to more unusual combinations, giving regular customers a reason to see what was being served on a particular day.

The restaurant's compact Midtown setting is another important part of its character. It became famous for a quick, organized ordering system and a set of rules designed to keep the line moving. That reputation grew far beyond New York after the real-life soup shop inspired the famous soup episode of Seinfeld, bringing the restaurant and Al Yeganeh's distinctive approach to a much wider audience.

One unusual chapter in the restaurant's history came after the original storefront closed in 2004. Yeganeh's recipes and brand were later used for the Original SoupMan business, while the famous 55th Street location eventually reopened. The shop now carries the name The Original Soup Kitchen, keeping a connection to the original New York soup counter.
