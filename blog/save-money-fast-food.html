How to Save Money at Fast Food Restaurants: 10 Practical Tips

Last Updated: August 2026

Eating out has gotten expensive. A quick meal that used to cost $5 now easily runs $9 or $10, and if you're ordering for a family, that adds up fast. The good news is that fast food chains still offer plenty of ways to eat well without spending more than you have to — you just need to know where to look.


Here are 10 practical, easy-to-use tips that can help you cut your fast food bill without cutting corners on your meal.


1. Download the App Before You Order

Almost every major fast food chain has its own app, and almost every app has deals you won't find at the counter. Chains like McDonald's, Taco Bell, Burger King, and Wendy's regularly offer app-only discounts — sometimes a free item just for signing up. If you order fast food even once a month, the app pays for itself in savings.


2. Sign Up for the Loyalty or Rewards Program

Most restaurant apps come with a built-in rewards program. You earn points on every purchase, and those points turn into free food over time. It costs nothing to join, and the rewards build up faster than most people expect, especially if you order from the same place regularly.


3. Check for a Value Menu or "$1 $2 $3" Menu

Nearly every fast food chain still has some version of a value menu, even if it's not advertised on the main board. Items like small fries, side salads, or a basic burger are often priced well below the combo meals. Ordering two or three value items can end up cheaper than one combo — and sometimes it's more food too.


4. Order Combos Instead of Building Your Own Meal

This one works the opposite way depending on what you're ordering. If you want a full meal — main item, side, and drink — a combo is almost always cheaper than buying each piece separately. Always compare the combo price to the sum of the individual items before you order.


5. Watch for Limited-Time Deals

Fast food chains run promotions throughout the year — buy-one-get-one offers, discounted meal bundles, and holiday specials. These deals aren't permanent, so it helps to check the app or the chain's website before you head out, especially if you're planning a bigger order for a family or group.


6. Skip the Delivery App Markup

Ordering through DoorDash, Uber Eats, or similar apps is convenient, but prices on these platforms are often marked up compared to ordering directly from the restaurant or its own app. If you're trying to save money, picking up the order yourself — or using the restaurant's own delivery option, if it has one — is usually the cheaper choice.


7. Order Water Instead of a Drink

Soda, coffee, and specialty drinks often carry the highest markup on any fast food menu. Skipping the drink and asking for water instead can shave a noticeable amount off your total, especially if you're ordering for more than one person.


8. Watch Portion Size vs. Price

Bigger isn't always better value. Sometimes a "large" size costs a dollar or two more than a medium for a small increase in food. Before upsizing, do a quick mental check — is the extra amount actually worth the extra cost, or are you just paying more for the same meal?


9. Use Restaurant Gift Cards Wisely

Gift cards are sometimes sold at a discount through warehouse clubs or cashback shopping sites — meaning you can get $25 of food for $20 or less. If you eat at the same chain often, buying gift cards this way is one of the easiest ways to save without changing how or where you eat.


10. Time Your Order Around Happy Hour or Off-Peak Deals

A number of chains offer discounted prices during specific hours — often mid-afternoon, when foot traffic is lower. These "happy hour" style deals can apply to drinks, snacks, or even full menu items. If your schedule is flexible, ordering during these windows is an easy way to pay less for the same food.


Final Thoughts

Saving money at fast food restaurants doesn't mean giving up convenience — it just means ordering a little smarter. A few small habits, like using the app, checking the value menu, and skipping the delivery markup, can add up to real savings over a month or a year. Start with one or two of these tips and build from there.
