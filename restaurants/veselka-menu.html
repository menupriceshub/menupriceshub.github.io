Overview

Veselka is a Ukrainian restaurant in the East Village of New York City, and anyone searching for Veselka menu prices is usually planning a big plate of comfort food. Pierogi, borscht, and classic diner dishes sit side by side on one long list.

The story starts in 1954, when Ukrainian immigrant Wolodymyr Darmohraj opened a small newsstand and candy shop on Second Avenue. Over the years it grew into a full restaurant and a neighborhood landmark. The name Veselka means "rainbow" in Ukrainian.

The Veselka menu is where that mix shows. Veselka pierogi come boiled or pan-fried, filled with potato and cheese, meat, or mushroom, and served with onions and sour cream. Borscht is earthy and slightly sweet from beets, and it can be served hot or cold. Stuffed cabbage is tender in tomato sauce, potato pancakes are crisp at the edges, and pancakes and egg plates cover the breakfast side.

Among Veselka New York restaurants, this one stands out for pairing a diner feel with home-style Ukrainian cooking. The room is casual, bright, and busy, with students, neighbors, and visitors sharing the space. It works for a group meal, a solo bowl of soup, or a slow brunch.
